# Prior art for the bidirectional MPPI–policy mechanism

The proposed loop is an actor-containing, GPS-inspired mechanism test. Alternating local control and global policy learning, policy–planner agreement, learned long-horizon guidance, and bidirectional planning/learning feedback already have substantial precedent. The present implementation does not establish a new RL algorithm, a unique combination, convergence, standalone competence, or an efficiency advantage. This note maps seven close primary papers to the actual mechanisms; it is not an exhaustive novelty search.

| Primary work | Established mechanism relevant here | Distinction that this implementation must test |
|---|---|---|
| Levine, Finn, Darrell & Abbeel, **End-to-End Training of Deep Visuomotor Policies** (JMLR 2016; 2015 preprint), [paper, §4](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume17/15-522/15-522.pdf) | BADMM GPS alternates trajectory-controller optimization and supervised global-policy fitting, with agreement expressed through KL terms and dual updates. The paper also initially trains local controllers independently until they attain basic competence (§3). Independent initial acquisition is therefore established. | Here the continuing private MPPI proposal stream remains independent after guidance becomes available. The actor additionally receives return credit from its own complete executions. Empirical promotion/revocation is not a BADMM dual update and inherits no BADMM guarantee. |
| Montgomery & Levine, **Guided Policy Search via Approximate Mirror Descent** (NeurIPS 2016), [paper, Algorithm 1 and §§2–4](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/file/a00e5eb0973d24649a4a920fc53d9564-Paper.pdf) | GPS already adapts local optimization to the global policy and treats supervised fitting as an approximate projection. Its analysis distinguishes exact simplified cases from approximation error; matching teacher actions alone does not ensure the student's long-horizon performance. | Actor-only completion tests and paired executed-replanning tests are operational evidence gates. They do not implement the paper's mirror-descent projection or justify its theoretical bounds for this MPPI/actor procedure. |
| Lowrey et al., **Plan Online, Learn Offline: Efficient Learning and Exploration via Model-Based Control** (ICLR 2019), [paper, §§2–3 and appendix](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.01848) | POLO explicitly combines local trajectory optimization, global value learning and temporally coordinated exploration; its examples use MPPI. Learned terminal values provide information beyond the short planning horizon. Coverage and value approximation are substantive assumptions, not solved by writing a Bellman target. | The current global object is an action policy, not POLO's value ensemble. Its long-term return samples evaluate the identified behavior actor's continuation. A value-guided, actor-free pilot would test a different link and would not constitute a standalone-policy GPS result. |
| Sikchi, Zhou & Held, **Learning Off-Policy with Online Planning** (CoRL/PMLR 2022), [paper, §5.1 and Eq. 6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v164/sikchi22a/sikchi22a.pdf) | LOOP combines finite-horizon planning with an off-policy learned terminal value. ARC explicitly addresses divergence between the collection planner and the actor used in value backups, constraining trajectory selection toward an actor-derived prior. Policy regularization of local search is established. | This implementation keeps acquisition proposals/private search state independent and gates a separate compatibility cost using measured outcomes. That structural choice is different from an actor-derived proposal prior; whether it helps remains empirical. |
| Hansen, Wang & Su, **Temporal Difference Learning for Model Predictive Control** (ICML 2022), [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/hansen22a.html), [authors' implementation](https://github.com/nicklashansen/tdmpc/blob/main/src/algorithm/tdmpc.py) | TD-MPC jointly learns a task-oriented latent model and terminal value for short-horizon control. Its implementation mixes learned-policy trajectories with sampled plans, and trains the policy using learned Q estimates (`plan`, `estimate_value`, `update_pi`). Joint actors, critics and online trajectory optimization are established. | The present simulator supplies dynamics; complete actor-owned outcomes supply actor credit. The actor does not seed the retained independent proposal bank. These differences remove particular target/proposal dependencies, not the need for coverage or competent optimization. |
| Zhan et al., **Bootstrap Off-policy with World Model (BOOM)** (NeurIPS 2025; retrieved arXiv v3, 2026), [paper, §§2.2–3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.00423) | This is especially close: the policy initializes an MPPI planner, and planner-generated actions feed policy alignment. A jointly learned world model/value supports both; the alignment term is weighted using Q estimates. Thus the broad policy→planner→policy bootstrap loop and handling variable planner-action quality are already explicit prior art. | Here auxiliary teacher supervision is allowed, while complete return targets remain attached to the executing actor. Influence begins absent, and successful suffix handovers establish only eligibility; paired complete replanning tests must earn deployment influence. These are testable protocol choices, not proof of superiority over BOOM. |
| Boyalakuntla, Liu & Boularias, **Beyond Policy Alignment: Closing the Planning-Learning Loop for Robot Control with Learned World Models** (September 2026 preprint), [primary abstract and paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39751) | The recent PL-MPC preprint explicitly frames planning and learning as a feedback loop. It combines multi-step TD supervision, disagreement-aware terminal values and return-weighted planner-action distillation; reported gains are task dependent. This already covers broad claims about repairing feedback rather than merely aligning actions. | The present trust evidence concerns actual policy-only suffix completion and complete executed replanning, rather than critic disagreement alone. No claim that this exact evidence gate is unique is warranted by this limited search, and the preprint's results do not establish this implementation's outcomes. |

The implementation's narrow hypothesis is that full successful trajectory families can supply reusable supervision and reset contexts, while actor-owned complete returns preserve the actor's long-term objective. An explicit family input can represent alternatives without forcing incompatible actions into one conditional mean; it does not guarantee that the actor learns any complete family. Successful teacher states alone establish neither actor competence nor a causal curriculum benefit.

In the reverse direction, a version/task/family/time/support-specific record starts without influence. Complete actor suffixes can make it eligible; only paired complete executions of the baseline and guided replanning controllers can promote a compatibility weight. Revocation and version invalidation return its weight to zero. Within qualified support, reranking may favor actor-compatible candidates, and any newly averaged plan must receive its own model query. The protected independent bank, raw objective and private planner memory remain available for comparison. Finite empirical qualification still assumes some generalization between measured support anchors; it is not a formal safety certificate or a guarantee for every deployment state.

The contribution presently sought is a falsifiable, auditable integration: explicit continuation ownership, separate discovery and learning, family-consistent supervision, protected independent proposals, measured and revocable influence, and complete raw-cost accounting. Each is a design constraint to evaluate. Broad bidirectionality, imitation from optimized trajectories, terminal-value MPC, or calling a coefficient “trust” is not a novelty claim. Successful execution, standalone actor performance, beneficial guidance, and all-cost exploration/sample efficiency remain separate empirical claims; a negative result must survive unchanged.

Primary PDFs/author sources were consulted for the first six entries; the final recent preprint's primary abstract was checked. No simulation, actor inference, training or new experiment was performed for this note. No particular critic architecture, BC-only objective, SAC, or novelty bonus is required by this map.

The prospective Ending v3 revision additionally uses classical one-step rollout: compare a candidate first action followed by a complete frozen base-policy continuation, include the base policy's action, and execute only the chosen first action. [Bertsekas, *Multiagent Reinforcement Learning: Rollout and Policy Iteration* (2021)](https://stuff.mit.edu/~dimitrib/Multiagent_Sinica_2020.pdf) describes rollout and its base-policy improvement property. This component is established prior art. Its conditional deterministic improvement argument does not establish learned-policy improvement, model accuracy, empirical support generalization or efficiency for the present pipeline. The v3 protocol separately tests executed control against the base actor and matched subsequent student learning.
